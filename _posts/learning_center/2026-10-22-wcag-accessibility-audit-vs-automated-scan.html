An automated accessibility scan and a WCAG accessibility audit can both help a team find website barriers, but they do different jobs. A scan checks pages for issues that software can detect. An audit combines automated results with human evaluation against an agreed scope and criteria.

Knowing the difference helps organizations choose an appropriate first step, interpret results, and plan follow-up work.

What an automated accessibility scan does

An automated scan checks page code and rendered content associated with accessibility issues. WAVE-powered scans, for example, can flag items such as missing form labels, empty links, some color contrast problems, and structural concerns for someone to review.

Scan results are useful for finding potential issues across many pages and tracking changes over time. They still need interpretation: some flags may not be barriers in context, and a scan may miss problems that depend on meaning, interaction, or a person’s experience.

An automated scan by itself does not establish that a website conforms to WCAG or ADA requirments. A clean scan result is not proof that all accessibility barriers have been found.

What a manual WCAG Evaluation audit adds

A manual evaluation examines the website within a defined scope. The evaluator can assess context and interactions that automated checks cannot fully judge, such as whether instructions make sense, whether keyboard use works through a task, and whether controls behave accessibly.

At Kaffeinated Kodemonkey, scoped audits combine WAVE-powered scans with manual testing following the DHS Trusted Tester Process. The agreed scope may identify pages, templates, features, user journeys, and applicable WCAG criteria. The report records what was evaluated and the findings; it should not be interpreted as a guarantee about pages or situations outside that scope.

Scan or audit: which should your team use?

An automated scan can be a practical way to establish a baseline or keep watch for detectable issues as a site changes. It can help developers and content teams prioritize items for review.

A scoped audit is more appropriate when an organization needs documented evaluation findings, manual assessment, remediation guidance, or accessibility documentation for internal planning or procurement. Teams often use scans and manual evaluation together because they provide different kinds of information.

Need Automated scan Scoped WEAR audit
Check many configured pages for detectable patterns Yes May include scans as one method
Interpret issues in context Limited Yes, within agreed scope
Evaluate interactions manually No Yes, within agreed scope
Document methods, scope, and findings Scan results Audit report and agreed deliverables
