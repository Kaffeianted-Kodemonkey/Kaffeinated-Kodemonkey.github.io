Kaffeinated Kodemonkey designs and develops websites with accessibility considered throughout planning, design, and implementation. We work with organizations to define project goals, audiences, technical requirements, and an appropriate accessibility scope before work begins.

Founded by a DHS Certified Trusted Tester with extensive experience in web development and digital accessibility, Kaffeinated Kodemonkey brings accessibility and front-end expertise to new website projects and redesigns.

Accessibility-focused design and development can include semantic page structure, keyboard-operable interactions, clear visual hierarchy, and attention to color contrast. The exact work and testing methods depend on the project. Automated scans can help identify some detectable issues; manual checks, including keyboard and assistive technology testing or the DHS Trusted Tester Process, may be included when appropriate to the agreed scope.

Plan for Accessibility From the Start

Considering accessibility early helps teams address user needs while design and implementation decisions are being made. We collaborate with your team to align the work with your content, platform, audience, and project constraints.

  • Project discovery: Define audiences, goals, platform needs, and accessibility requirements.
  • Accessible design: Consider readable content, color contrast, focus visibility, and clear interaction patterns.
  • Development: Build with semantic HTML and accessible interaction patterns that fit the selected platform and project scope.
  • Evaluation: Use agreed automated and manual checks to review the delivered work and document findings within the evaluation scope.
  • Optional documentation: WCAG reports, VPAT/Accessibility Conformance Reports, or Proof of Compliance Certificate (POCC) materials may be scoped separately where appropriate.

How a Project Works

  1. Scope the work: Discuss the website, content, functionality, timeline, and accessibility objectives.
  2. Design and build: Develop the agreed pages and features with accessibility considered across the project.
  3. Review and refine: Perform the agreed testing, share findings, and address items included in the project scope.
  4. Discuss ongoing needs: Plan any additional evaluation, remediation, monitoring, or documentation as a separate next step if needed.
